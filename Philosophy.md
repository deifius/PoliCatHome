# The Philosophy of PoliC@home

> **No PolyC without PoliC. Culpability with clarity.**

PoliC@home begins with a modest proposition: computation belongs, first, to
whoever controls the machine that performs it. Spare capacity is not an
unclaimed natural resource. It can be offered, but it cannot be presumed.

We hope to build a distributed computational commons whose participants can
cooperate without surrendering their autonomy. That requires a separation
between **freedom to build software** and **permission to use another person's
compute**.

## Freedom to build, including the freedom to keep

The PoliC@home software is released under the permissive BSD 3-Clause license.
People may study it, copy it, adapt it, sell services built on it, fork it,
and keep their own improvements private or proprietary. Contributions back to the project
are welcome, but never a condition of using the software.

A person who develops a better scheduler, a quieter Catnap interface, or an
entirely new implementation does not acquire a debt to the original Commons.
Reciprocity has value when freely offered; compulsory reciprocity is not the
kind of institution we are trying to build.

This also means forks may pursue goals we disagree with. The software license
does not authorize us to govern every derivative's behavior. What we *can*
do is choose which projects to trust, which services to federate with, and
which uses of **our own resources** we will accept.

## No consent by proximity

Visiting a website is not consent to donate computation. Downloading code is
not consent to run it. Enrolling is not the same as starting Catnap.

**PoliC — Candid Constitutional Consent** — describes the conditions under
which a contributor is willing to participate. A person should be able to
inspect those conditions in ordinary language and in machine-readable form,
start work deliberately, pause locally, revoke authorization, and remove the
installed worker and its credentials.

A Cat (a submitted computational project) has its own terms and requirements.
A Catbox is the execution environment; it is not an additional owner of the
computer. Cooperation occurs only at the intersection of compatible, current
constitutions. No coordinator can grant itself permission that the compute
owner has withheld.

## Coordination without ownership

**PolyC — Coordinated Computational Care** — is the work of bringing
consenting people, machines, and Cats into useful cooperation.

The Commons should make commitments legible, distribute tasks fairly, record
what was actually verified, and withdraw work when consent changes. It should
not confuse successful coordination with entitlement to command. A
coordinator is useful precisely because its authority is limited and
revocable.

In cybernetic terms, we aim for cooperation between autonomous viable units,
not a single master machine whose local participants are merely peripherals.
The system should be capable of changing who coordinates it, and of surviving
when someone chooses to leave.

## Culpability requires clarity

A signed declaration is evidence of a declaration, not proof of the world's
truth. A recorded task completion is evidence of a result under a particular
verification method, not proof of benevolence, energy savings, or a fair
financial settlement. PoliC@home must distinguish measurement from inference,
and promises from implemented safeguards.

The current browser Catnap is limited to approved, built-in CPU tasks with
best-effort pacing. It is **not** a guaranteed temperature or power limiter.
The native Docker Catbox has stronger container resource limits but is not an
arbitrary untrusted-code VM. These limits deserve plain language, not
constitutional pageantry.

## A commons one may leave

Freedom includes saying *no*: no to a creator, no to a purpose, no to a
particular contract, no to a Commons, and no to continued participation.

It also includes saying *yes* to a private fork, a commercial service, or an
independent experiment. A permissive license and enforceable consent are not
contradictory. They operate at different boundaries.

**Freedom to build does not confer authority over another's machine.**
