# Art, humor, and rights

## Creative direction

PolyC@home takes inspiration from James Thurber’s dry domestic absurdism, and from Python’s tradition of light, recurring cultural references. PolyCats are independent contributors; Catnap is the act of contributing compute, while its screensaver visualizes measured work. Chronicle explains that work with occasional wit. Safety, consent, and audit logs must never be ambiguous.

## Thurber provenance to verify

The user identified a four-cat image and the “We have cats the way most people have mice” exchange as material collected in *The Thurber Carnival*, under “Joke Pets,” and linked a 1930 *New Yorker* “Our Pet Department” entry: https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1930/04/05/our-pet-department-4 . This link is a research lead, **not verified provenance for the exact drawing or exchange**.

US works actually published in 1930 are generally in the US public domain as of 2026, but a 1945 anthology may contain artwork added later. Before reproducing any specific image or text, document its earliest publication with page/issue evidence, check other applicable rights, and provide attribution. Do not add the supplied scan or any reproduction until verified or licensed.

## Original house style

- Loose, playful, observational humor; no copying a specific Thurber composition or distinctive character.
- No claim of affiliation with the Thurber estate, *The New Yorker*, or the Linux Foundation.
- New artwork should be original and committed with explicit license and creator credit.
- Example original line: “The cats have agreed to cooperate. They have not agreed on what that means.”

## HIRDing C@s editorial notes

The name salutes GNU Hurd's recursive HIRD/HURD wordplay, not its code or branding. Our own original domestic jokes should be observational, spare, and technically honest. Suggested original line: “A HIRD is not a herd. The distinction is most apparent at feeding time.” Do not reproduce a specific Thurber illustration or caption without verified rights.
